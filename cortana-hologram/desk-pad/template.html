How the light reaches her

One change from the concept: the projector still hides in the pad, but it now stands lens-up in a short rear tower and bounces off a mirror behind a sealed glass window. The light leaves the sloped face and crosses the mist sheet from behind and below.

  • Why: fog only shows an image when light crosses it, and it glows brightest towards the viewer when lit from behind. Shining straight up along the sheet would light a blur, not her.
  • The beam points away from your eyes, so you never look into the lens, and the spill lands harmlessly on the desk in front.
  • The folded path gives the projector the 25 to 30 cm of throw it needs for a 25 cm image without making the pad deeper.
  • Her software pre-warps the picture for the angle, the same way projectors do keystone correction, and the mirror tilt fine-tunes it.

Build it in three stages

Stage 1 · one evening of printing

Mist test

Print only the tank, lid, nozzle, honeycomb and fan ducts. Run the mist on the desk with the projector propped on books behind it. This proves the sheet is smooth and finds the best beam angle before the big shell parts are printed.

Stage 2 · about four days of printing

Body

Deck, bottom plate, tower, holo ring, cradle, mirror mount, trays and port panel. Wire the ESP32, fans, mister and LED ring, then fit the projector.

Stage 3 · about two days of printing

Collector

Spine, cap and cold-plate tray. The cap's fan and Peltier plate catch the mist above her head and drip it back to the tank, so the room stays dry and top-ups are rare.

Before anything prints, three things to decide

  • The light path above. Rear tower with mirror (recommended), or keep the original idea of a mirror in the base aimed straight up.
  • The projector. For a 25 cm Cortana it needs a short throw (about 0.8 or lower) and 500+ lumens. A normal pico projector also works, but she would come out about 16 cm tall. I'll shortlist models you can buy in Australia and size the cradle to the one you pick.
  • The footprint. The concept said 30 × 22 cm. This version is 25 × 25 cm so the deck and tower each print in one piece with no joins in the wet area.

Her figure in the viewer is a flat stand-in shape. Her real model never goes into these files. The parts here are original designs, so the print files can live in the project; anything made from her game model stays on your PC only.
