How you see her from every side

Looking down from above, four 15.6" portable screens lie face down in a pinwheel, one over each glass face, with their long sides running out from the centre. Each shows her rendered from its side. Each face reflects its own screen, and all four reflections meet on the pad in the middle, so whichever side you stand on, she is standing there seen from your side.

The pinwheel matters. Four screens can't all reach the exact centre, so each is shifted sideways and starts 6 cm out. That gap sits above her head, where the printed apex hub hides it, and it is the reason the unit is 92 cm across for a 31 cm figure.

  • She is still a live 3D model. Walk round and you see her back, her profile, then her face, and she can turn to follow you.
  • Each side has its own camera. The side you're looking through renders her from exactly where you stand, so the view stays smooth as you move along that face.
  • Where two faces meet there is a thin seam, and the view jumps by a quarter turn as you cross a corner. That is the limit of four faces. A smooth 360 would need a spinning or volumetric display.
  • Each screen is dedicated to one side, so she is as bright as in the front capsule. A dimmer room still suits it best, because you see the room through the far glass behind her.
  • Four screens need four video outputs. Your RTX 4070 has four (three DisplayPort and one HDMI), and portable screens take USB-C or mini HDMI, so one PC next to it can drive all four.

Bought parts

  • Four 15.6" portable monitors (about 357 × 223 × 10 mm each), the same model so the four views match, as bright as you can find.
  • Four beam-splitter acrylic trapezoids, 800 mm along the bottom, 120 mm at the top and 481 mm up the slope, 3 mm thick. At this size 3 mm teleprompter glass also works (about 2 kg each) and reflects a little more evenly.
  • Aluminium 2020 extrusion, about 18 m cut to length (four corner posts, three square rings, two cross rails, four screen beams), plus corner brackets and T-nuts.
  • Black aluminium composite for the four pedestal sides, the screen mask and the crown top, and 12 mm MDF for the deck, painted black.
  • Four small USB camera boards, a 3" speaker and amplifier, eight small mics, ESP32 with LED ring and strips, and four video cables to the PC.

Rough total: A$1,250 to A$2,300 (estimate). The four portable monitors (A$600 to A$1,200) and the beam-splitter panels (A$250 to A$500) are most of it. One person can lift it; it comes apart into crown, pillars, panels and pedestal.

The figure in the viewer is a plain stand-in shape. Her real model never goes into these files. The parts are original designs, and anything made from her game model stays on your PC only.
