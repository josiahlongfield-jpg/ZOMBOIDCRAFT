How you see her from every side

Looking down at the screen, it is split into four triangles, one above each glass face. Each triangle shows her rendered from that side: front, back, left and right. Each face reflects its own triangle, and all four reflections meet on the pad in the middle, so whichever side you stand on, she is standing there seen from your side.

  • She is still a live 3D model. Walk round and you see her back, her profile, then her face, and she can turn to follow you.
  • Each side has its own camera. The side you're looking through renders her from exactly where you stand, so the view stays smooth as you move along that face.
  • Where two faces meet there is a thin seam, and the view jumps by a quarter turn as you cross a corner. That is the limit of four faces. A smooth 360 would need a spinning or volumetric display.
  • Each view only gets a quarter of the screen, so she comes out about 20 cm tall, against 38 cm in the front-facing capsule. She is also less bright, so a dimmer room suits it.

Bought parts

  • LG DualUp 28MQ780 (481.5 × 549.5 × 45 mm without its stand, per LG). It lies face down in the crown.
  • Four beam-splitter glass triangles, about 460 mm along the base, with the tips cut off so they meet under a printed apex cap.
  • Four USB camera boards, a 4" speaker and amplifier, eight small mics, ESP32 with LED ring and strips, and four 10 mm steel rods.

Rough total: A$1,100 to A$1,700 (estimate). Most of it is the monitor (around A$700 to A$1,000) and the four glass panels.

The figure in the viewer is a plain stand-in shape. Her real model never goes into these files. The parts are original designs, and anything made from her game model stays on your PC only.
