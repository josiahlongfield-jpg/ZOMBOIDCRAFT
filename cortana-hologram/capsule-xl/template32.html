How she appears inside

A screen in the crown faces straight down. Sheet glass set at exactly 45 degrees reflects it towards you, so her picture seems to stand upright at the back of the capsule, right on top of the glowing pad. You still see through the glass, so she floats in the space instead of looking like a flat screen. The trick is called Pepper's ghost, and theatres have used it for over 150 years.

  • The inside is dark (smoked side panels and a black back), so only she glows. This is why it works in a lit room, unlike the mist.
  • Her picture is rendered on pure black, flipped for the mirror, and sized so her feet land on the pad.
  • The camera in the crown lets her turn to face you as you move, which makes her look more solid.
  • Teleprompter glass reflects about 30%, against about 8% for plain acrylic, so she comes out several times brighter. Use it.

Bought parts

  • 32" monitor (16:9, about 714 × 424 × 45 mm without its stand), as bright as you can find (400 nits or more), with a VESA mount. It lies face down in the crown with its long side running front to back, which is what gives her the 64 cm.
  • Beam-splitter (teleprompter) glass, about 440 × 992 mm, in one sheet (a join would run straight through her). 6 mm glass weighs about 6.5 kg; beam-splitter acrylic is a lighter option that reflects a little less evenly.
  • Two smoked acrylic side panels of 828 × 714 × 3 mm and a matte black back panel of about 470 × 714 mm.
  • USB camera board, 4" full-range speaker + small amplifier, two small mics, ESP32 + LED ring and strip, and four 12 mm steel rods, about 75 cm long, through the pillars.

Rough total for the bought parts: A$850 to A$1,600 (estimate), most of it the monitor and the large glass sheet. I've added this version to your parts checklist.

Choices for you

  • Size. It's 94 cm tall and 86 cm deep, because the glass has to run her full 64 cm height at 45 degrees. That suits a floor stand or a deep desk more than a normal desk. It weighs roughly 25 to 30 kg built (glass, screen and prints), so it moves in two pieces: crown and body.
  • Front or all sides. This version shows her from the front only. A 360 version at this height needs four screens and is about 1.4 m wide; I can draw it too if you want.
  • Look. Forge-style chamfered pillars with glowing edge lines, a light bar on the base, and a ring pad she stands on. All of it is original, not Razer's cylinder.
  • The front. I left it open, so nothing gets between you and her. A clear front panel can go in to keep dust out, but it adds one faint reflection.
